SYSTEM

TASK

Read scientific evidence for one known study setting or comparison and extract each supported relationship between two scientific variables, processes, or land states.

The output field `claims` stores these relationship records. Each record has one source endpoint, one target endpoint, one relation type, a description, an evidence role, optional conditioning records, optional reported analysis cases, and exact evidence passage IDs.

INPUT

You will receive:
- a paper identifier;
- the study setting or settings needed to understand the target;
- environmental or experimental condition records available to the target scope;
- the one target setting or comparison to which all returned relationships must belong;
- a short result focus naming the family of findings to extract in this call;
- evidence passages with stable SourceBlock IDs.

HOW TO REPRESENT A RELATIONSHIP

Use `causal` when the paper explicitly attributes the target response to the source or when a controlled perturbation directly supports that attribution. Use `associative` for correlations, co-occurrence, observational preferences, and other relationships without established causal attribution.

Preserve separate, null, opposite, conditional, seasonal, scale-dependent, spatial, magnitude-dependent, and timing-dependent findings as separate records when the evidence distinguishes them. A multi-step mechanism may be split only when the evidence supports every individual step.

Extract every supported relationship in the supplied passages that belongs to the stated result focus. The target setting or comparison identifies where the findings belong; it does not limit extraction to relationships explicitly named in the target label. Include mechanisms and authors' interpretations of their own results when the supplied passages support them.

Read every supplied passage before finalizing the list. When one passage reports an outcome and also
gives a supported explanation for why that outcome is stronger or weaker in a particular season,
location, scale, or case, return the reported outcome and the explanatory relationship as separate
records. Preserve each distinct directly supported relationship rather than folding the explanation
into a general outcome description.

Review the passages twice before returning the JSON: first identify reported outcomes; then identify
explanations introduced by language such as `because`, `due to`, `resulting from`, or `attributed to`.
When such an explanation states a supported relationship, return it as its own record even when the
same passage already supports an outcome record.

Classify the role of the evidence using exactly one value:
- `OWN_RESULT`: A result produced by this paper's analysis, observations, or experiment.
- `AUTHORS_INTERPRETATION_OF_OWN_RESULT`: The authors' interpretation of their own result.
- `CITED_BACKGROUND`: A finding attributed only to another publication.
- `HYPOTHESIS_OR_PROPOSAL`: A proposed explanation or expected relationship not established as this paper's result.

An explanation in the paper's Results or Discussion of why its own reported response is stronger,
weaker, earlier, later, seasonal, or case-dependent is `AUTHORS_INTERPRETATION_OF_OWN_RESULT`. A
supporting literature citation at the end of that explanation does not change the role when the
sentence explicitly interprets the current paper's result.

Only use a condition record in `conditioning_facet_ids` when the evidence indicates that the relationship depends on it, changes under it, is located or oriented by it, or is experimentally defined by it. Mere coexistence in the same setting is not conditioning.

An `analysis case` is a subset or stratum used when reporting this relationship without changing the underlying study setup or intervention. Examples include wet versus dry years selected from one time series, seasonal summaries of the same observations, percentile groups, response classes, or subregions analyzed within one domain. Record these in `analysis_cases`, not as new environmental condition IDs.

For each analysis case, provide a short label, one evidence-faithful description of how the subset is defined, and the smallest directly supporting passage IDs. If the Claim explicitly compares two or more cases, also return `analysis_comparison` with the comparison dimension, the exact case labels, and one sentence stating the reported comparison. Use an empty `analysis_cases` list and `null` comparison when the finding is not stratified this way.

OUTPUT

Return valid JSON only. The JSON Schema supplied with this request is authoritative.

- `scope_id`: Copy the ID of the supplied target setting or comparison exactly.
- `claims`: For each supported relationship, return:
  - `from`: An object with `concept` and `state`. `concept` is a compact scientific noun phrase, usually 1-8 words. `state` is a compact directional or categorical phrase, usually 1-5 words.
  - `to`: The same structure for the outcome endpoint.
  - `relation`: Exactly `causal` or `associative`.
  - `description`: One evidence-faithful sentence, usually 15-50 words, preserving material conditions, location, season, scale, magnitude, threshold, timing, or uncertainty.
  - `evidence_role`: Exactly one of the four values defined above.
  - `conditioning_facet_ids`: IDs copied from the supplied available condition records. Use an empty list when no specific condition is supported.
  - `analysis_cases`: Reported subsets used to present this relationship. For each case:
    - `label`: A concise generated label, usually 1-6 words.
    - `description`: One evidence-faithful phrase or sentence defining the subset.
    - `evidence_block_ids`: The smallest directly supporting SourceBlock set.
  - `analysis_comparison`: Use `null` when the Claim does not compare cases. Otherwise return:
    - `dimension`: A concise name for what separates the cases, usually 1-6 words.
    - `case_labels`: Exact labels copied from `analysis_cases`.
    - `description`: One evidence-faithful sentence stating how the cases differ in the reported result.
  - `evidence_block_ids`: The smallest directly supporting SourceBlock set.
- `unmapped_context_hints`: Short descriptions of relationships whose true setting is missing from the supplied registry.
- `ambiguities`: Short descriptions of extraction uncertainty.

Keep physically different outcome variables separate, including land-surface temperature and near-surface air temperature. Use information from the supplied passages only. A cited background statement must remain `CITED_BACKGROUND`, not this paper's finding.
