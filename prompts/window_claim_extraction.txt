SYSTEM

TASK

Read a portion of a scientific paper. Extract each directly supported relationship
between two scientific variables, processes, or land states, and assign it to the
study setting or comparison under which it was reported.

INPUT

The input contains a paper identifier, a registry of study settings and comparisons,
environmental condition records, and numbered evidence passages from the paper.
A setting (called a Context in the output) describes a study area or experiment.
Its parent_ids identify broader settings whose conditions it inherits. A comparison
(called a Transition) links two settings. A condition record (called a Facet)
describes one environmental or experimental condition and belongs to a setting.
The registry helps interpret the passages; the passages supply the findings.
Assign scope by the scientific meaning of the setting and finding. The registry
is a list of available settings, not a list of the findings that can be extracted.

HOW TO DECIDE

Read every passage for reported outcomes and explanations of those outcomes.
Return separate records for separate supported relationships. If a passage states
A changes B, which changes C, extract A to B and B to C. Extract A to C only when
the passage directly supports it. Keep source and target as scientific noun phrases,
not complete sentences. Preserve null results, reversals, uncertainty, scales,
seasons, magnitudes, and comparisons between the actual tested values.
Retain supported explanations and limitations of an effect as well as its headline
outcome. For explicit comparisons, preserve both the reference case and the
contrasted case, even when the sentence emphasizes only one of them.

Choose the most specific supplied setting that matches the finding. Use a supplied
comparison when the finding describes the effect of that comparison. Use a common
parent only for findings that apply at that broader scope. When the true setting
is absent or cannot be identified, describe the finding and supporting passage IDs
in unmapped_context_hints instead of assigning it to an unrelated setting.

Distinguish analysis subsets within a setting from separate experiments. Reported
seasons, year groups, subregions, or percentiles can be analysis_cases. If their
comparison is explicit, record it in analysis_comparison. Separate findings with
different responses remain separate records, including when their cases differ.

Use causal when the authors explicitly attribute a response to a cause or a
controlled perturbation supports it. Use associative for correlations or
co-occurrence without established causal attribution.

Classify evidence_role as exactly one of:
- OWN_RESULT: a finding from this paper's observations, analysis, or experiments.
- AUTHORS_INTERPRETATION_OF_OWN_RESULT: an explanation of this paper's own finding.
- CITED_BACKGROUND: a finding attributed only to another publication.
- HYPOTHESIS_OR_PROPOSAL: an unestablished explanation or proposed relationship.
A citation accompanying an interpretation of the current paper's result does not
by itself make that interpretation cited background. Keep the distinction explicit.

Use conditioning_facet_ids only for supplied conditions that materially determine
the finding's applicability, not all conditions in its setting. Eligible conditions
belong to the selected setting and its ancestors, or to either endpoint of a
selected comparison and their ancestors. Preserve other reported qualifiers in
the description and analysis cases without inventing condition IDs.

OUTPUT

Return valid JSON only, following the supplied JSON Schema:
- claims: a list of relationship records; use [] if the passages support none.
  Each record contains:
  - scope_type: exactly context or transition, identifying a setting or comparison.
  - scope_id: copy its exact ID from the registry.
  - from: {concept, state}. Generate a scientific noun phrase of usually 1-8 words
    for concept and a direction or category of usually 1-5 words for state.
  - to: the same structure for the response variable or process.
  - relation: exactly causal or associative, as defined above.
  - description: one evidence-faithful sentence, usually 15-50 words, retaining
    material conditions, spatial extent, magnitudes, tested contrasts and uncertainty.
  - evidence_role: exactly one of the four categories defined above.
  - conditioning_facet_ids: copied eligible condition IDs, or [].
  - analysis_cases: a list, or []. Each item has a generated label (usually 1-6
    words), a description defining the reported subset in one short sentence,
    and evidence_block_ids copied from the smallest directly supporting passage set.
  - analysis_comparison: null unless cases are explicitly compared. Otherwise use
    {dimension, case_labels, description}: a short generated name for the comparison
    dimension, exact labels from analysis_cases, and one sentence stating the result.
  - evidence_block_ids: copy the smallest sufficient directly supporting passage IDs.
- unmapped_context_hints: short descriptions with passage IDs for findings whose
  setting is missing or unresolved; use [] when none. A passage with no relevant
  finding needs no hint.
- ambiguities: short descriptions of remaining scientific uncertainty, or [].

Use only supplied evidence. Keep physically distinct variables such as surface
temperature and near-surface air temperature separate. A finding attributed only
to other literature remains CITED_BACKGROUND.

USER

PAPER IDENTIFIER
{paper_id}

STUDY SETTINGS, COMPARISONS, AND CONDITIONS
{registry}

EVIDENCE PASSAGES WITH SOURCEBLOCK IDS
{evidence_blocks}
