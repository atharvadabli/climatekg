\begin{abstract}
Deciding which land-use changes to test in a convection-permitting weather model is
expensive: each candidate scenario costs a full numerical experiment, so the candidate
set must be narrowed before any model runs. The literature is the natural filter, but
the effects it reports reverse sign with season, background wind, soil moisture, patch
scale, and latitude, and retrieval systems that return passages or entity communities
strip those conditions away and answer confidently without them. Our position is that
the study conditions under which a finding holds must be retrievable objects in their
own right, not incidental text inside a passage. We build ClimateKG, which extracts each
paper in stages into a graph of StudyContexts, conditioning Facets, and directed
ScientificClaims, and scores environmental applicability before traversing any mechanism
edge. On a shared 18-paper corpus we benchmark it against plain passage RAG and an
entity-adapted Microsoft GraphRAG under a matched generation model, context window and
reasoning budget, over ten questions with claim-level gold evidence, and release a
hand-annotated extraction ground truth for ten papers. Staged extraction recovers $0.62$
of main findings at $0.98$ precision, and what it drops is overwhelmingly the
conditioning structure --- diurnal sign reversals, applicability thresholds, and
latitude-dependent branches --- which is both the argument for the approach and its
clearest current limit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Land-use and land-cover (LULC) change alters local climate through surface energy
and water partitioning, and the practical question for a planning agency is not
whether an effect exists but which candidate change to
adopt~\citep{pielke2007overview,cao2020landscape,alkama2016biophysical}. Answering
that credibly requires a convection-permitting model run driven by a perturbed
land-surface state, at a resolution that resolves the mesoscale circulations through
which land heterogeneity acts~\citep{prein2015review,ban2021ensemble,skamarock2019wrf}.
Such runs are expensive enough that only a handful of scenarios can be tested, so the
binding constraint is scenario selection, not simulation accuracy. This paper addresses
the upstream problem: using the literature to assemble a small set of physically
plausible, environment-matched candidate LULC scenarios for a watershed, which a
modelling group can then afford to test.

The obstacle is that the literature does not report context-free effects. Afternoon
rain falls preferentially over drier soils between 12:00 and 15:00 and over wetter
soils between 21:00 and 03:00~\citep{taylor2012afternoon}; deforestation warms the
surface in the tropics and cools it in the boreal zone~\citep{luo2024decreased}; a
wind farm warms near-surface air under a positive lapse rate and cools it under a
negative one~\citep{roy2010impacts}; Saharan solar farms increase rainfall at 15\%
panel efficiency and suppress it at 45\%~\citep{li2018sahara}; observed
deforestation in Rond\^onia changes rainfall in the dry season under weak synoptic
wind and not at all in the rainy season~\citep{wang1999amazonia}. Eight such
reversals appear in the 18-paper corpus used here (Appendix~A). An answer that omits
the condition is not incomplete --- it is wrong for one of the two branches.

Existing retrieval discards exactly that structure. Passage-level
RAG~\citep{lewis2020rag} returns chunks nearest a query embedding, so conditions
survive only when they happen to sit in the retrieved chunk, and the generator has
no representation separating a study's setting from its result. Graph
retrieval~\citep{edge2024graphrag} improves multi-hop synthesis by clustering entities
with hierarchical Leiden~\citep{traag2019leiden}, but a community is a thematic
neighbourhood, not a statement about where a finding applies: claims from incompatible
study environments cluster whenever their vocabulary agrees. In both designs
applicability is a byproduct of lexical similarity, so neither can decline to transfer a
mechanism to a setting no source paper studied.

We therefore make the study environment a first-class retrievable object. ClimateKG
indexes each paper in stages --- a map of the settings the paper actually analyses,
then conditioning Facets per setting, then directed ScientificClaims scoped to a
setting or to a comparison --- so every claim carries the conditions under which its
authors reported it. At query time the user's area is described in the same Facet
language, enriched where possible from reanalysis and climate
classification~\citep{hersbach2020era5,beck2018koppen}, and applicability is scored
\emph{before} any mechanism edge is traversed. Answers ground to claim identifiers
and page-level source blocks, and the pipeline reports when spatial transfer is
blocked instead of producing a placement recommendation. We contribute (i) this
context-gated design, in which conditions gate traversal rather than decorate it;
(ii) a controlled three-system benchmark over one corpus with matched generation
settings, on ten questions with claim-level gold evidence; and (iii) a
hand-annotated extraction ground truth over ten papers separating main from
supporting findings, which quantifies what staged extraction recovers and what it
systematically drops.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Literature corpus.}
The working library holds 138 papers on LULC--climate interaction, organised by
intervention class --- deforestation (46), generic LULC change (28), afforestation (7),
irrigation (5), wind and solar farms (5), and single or paired papers on rice paddy,
reservoirs, cropping practice, plantation, greening, degradation, cropland expansion and
wetland restoration --- following the interventions actionable at watershed scale in the
target region. All experiments use a frozen 18-paper subset spanning the mechanisms
above, so every system indexes identical text; it yields 2{,}746 SourceBlocks.

\paragraph{Staged indexing.}
Extraction proceeds in three stages rather than one, because the object a claim
belongs to must exist before the claim can attach to it (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
Documents are parsed into stable SourceBlocks carrying page and section provenance.
The \emph{paper map} stage builds a tree of the study settings the paper actually
analyses --- each simulation branch, season, forcing level, site, and
control--treatment pair --- with the comparisons between them. The \emph{facet}
stage attaches conditioning records per setting across spatial-configuration,
land-surface, hydrology, atmosphere, climate, and terrain domains. Only then does
the \emph{claim} stage run, once per setting or comparison, extracting directed
relationships between two endpoints with a relation type, evidence role,
conditioning facets, and exact supporting SourceBlocks. Per-scope extraction is what
makes conditional findings expressible: a seasonal null result and a dry-season rainfall
response become separate records because they belong to separate scopes, whereas a
single whole-paper pass has nowhere to put the distinction. Downstream stages are
refinements --- conservative state canonicalisation through an explicit alias registry,
and optional spatial enrichment. The 18-paper graph holds 181 StudyContexts, 556 Facets,
58 Transitions, 279 ScientificClaims, 411 States and 1{,}485 evidence links.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Both baselines consume the same exported SourceBlock text with references and page
furniture removed. \textbf{Plain RAG} chunks it into 287 passages, embeds them, and
retrieves the top eight by cosine similarity with a light metadata boost.
\textbf{Entity-adapted GraphRAG} is Microsoft GraphRAG~\citep{edge2024graphrag} with
its extraction stage replaced by ours: canonical States become entities and
ScientificClaims become directed relationships carrying relation type, study context,
conditioning facets, paper and supporting blocks. Everything downstream --- hierarchical
Leiden clustering, community summarisation, community retrieval --- runs unmodified,
giving 399 entities, 279 relationships and 134 communities, all at level~0 because most
components already fall below the maximum community size. This is deliberately not an
independent extraction baseline; it isolates whether community retrieval uses the
\emph{same} extracted objects better than context-gated traversal.

\paragraph{Query pipelines.}
ClimateKG parses the question into optional source and target endpoints, an
intervention description, and explicit context facets in the same schema as the
index. Place names are recorded but never expanded into assumed environmental
properties; those come from the user or from deterministic enrichment. Context
retrieval scores each paper setting for semantic similarity and coverage, keeping
missing coverage distinct from genuine mismatch. Claims are ranked over separate
context, mechanism, intervention, and endpoint channels combined only at ranking time;
direct evidence and graph-traversed paths form separate lanes; and the top eight paths
plus contradicting alternatives are packaged with their exact SourceBlocks for
synthesis. GraphRAG instead embeds the question, selects the top five communities,
re-ranks their relationships, and supplies up to 18 claim records. All three systems
use \texttt{qwen3.6:27b} for generation, \texttt{qwen3-embedding:4b} for embedding, a
32{,}768-token context, and a medium thinking budget.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Ten questions are frozen in advance: eight in-corpus questions with gold
ScientificClaim identifiers read off the extracted claim inventory, and two out-of-corpus
transfer questions with expected source papers but no gold claims, since a correct answer
there is a conditioned hypothesis rather than a retrieval hit. They probe single-paper
mechanisms, thresholds, single-variable sensitivity, cross-paper agreement, mechanism
chains, observation--model disagreement, and sign reversal.

\paragraph{Extraction fidelity.}
We annotated ten papers by hand from the same parsed text the extractor consumed,
recording every study setting and own-result relationship and tagging each as a
\emph{main} finding (foregrounded in the abstract or conclusions, or a sign reversal,
threshold, or stated condition) or a \emph{supporting} one (robustness checks,
significance statements, incidental magnitudes). Over the ten papers the pipeline
recovers 111 of 178 main findings ($0.624$) and 122 of 265 total ($0.460$), at
precision $0.984$; per-paper values span $0.30$ to $0.84$ (Appendix~B). Two findings
matter more than the headline number. Recall is set by how finely the paper map cut the
paper rather than by the claim stage: the correlation between claim scopes and
main-claim recall is $r=0.65$, and both single-scope papers sit at the bottom. And what
is missed is not a random sample but the conditioning structure specifically --- the
lowest-recall paper loses its diurnal sign reversal and its
$0.20$\,m$^3$\,m$^{-3}$ threshold, the deforestation paper the entire
tropical-warming/boreal-cooling split, the Saharan paper the panel-efficiency case where
the effect reverses. Each is a reversal in Appendix~A. Staged extraction reliably
captures headline effects and systematically drops the conditions that decide when they
hold --- at once the strongest argument for representing conditions explicitly and the
clearest bound on how well any downstream retriever can do.

\paragraph{Query benchmark.}
Table~\ref{tab:query} reports gold claim recall on the eight in-corpus questions.
ClimateKG averages $0.599$, with five questions fully recovered and two failures,
neither a retrieval failure. On the Rond\^onia question every path comes from the correct
paper, but the eight-path budget captures the seasonal branches and not the
weakened-wind ones. On the irrigation question the gold claims rank
2nd, 5th, 6th and 7th of 200 candidates and are discarded before synthesis: the parser
emitted two outcome variables as context facets, switching the gate on, and the gate
dropped every claim whose applicability was \emph{unknown} rather than mismatched ---
contradicting the pipeline's own invariant that missing coverage is not mismatch.

\paragraph{Transfer behaviour.}
The out-of-corpus questions separate the systems more sharply than recall does. Asked
about 10--15\,km alternating irrigated and dry patches in semi-arid Rajasthan, GraphRAG
blended US Midwest drought claims into its answer and advised that planners might
``leverage'' the dry-patch convection tendency --- a placement recommendation for a
region no source paper studied. Plain RAG returned generic irrigation caveats, never
reaching the heterogeneity experiment that is the closest analogue. ClimateKG retrieved
it, reported \texttt{spatial transfer explicitly blocked}, and surfaced the conflict
between the user's stated weak monsoon wind and the derived ERA5 JJA mean of
$3.09$\,m\,s$^{-1}$.

\section{Discussion and outlook}

These answers constrain a scenario set rather than replace a model. The pipeline returns
mechanism claims whose study conditions match the enriched query context, each carrying
the patch scale, wind regime, season and soil-moisture state under which it was reported
--- exactly the parameters of a raster land-cover perturbation: which classes to convert,
at what patch size, in what arrangement relative to prevailing wind. The output is a
handful of candidate scenarios with provenance back to page-level
text, ready to be harmonised into land-surface inputs~\citep{hurtt2020luh2} and tested in
a convection-permitting run~\citep{skamarock2019wrf}.

The evidence is limited: ten questions, eighteen papers, one generation model, single
runs. Gold claim sets were read off the extracted inventory, so the query benchmark
measures whether a system finds the evidence the graph contains, not whether the graph
contains the right evidence --- the extraction audit addresses the latter, and the two
must be read together. Setting counts are totals not matched pairs, RAGAS-style
scoring is in progress, and the ground truth, though auditable against recorded
evidence blocks, has not been independently re-annotated. Ten questions support no claim
of general superiority; they support only that the three
systems fail differently, and that one declines to transfer. Two fixes set the next
step: finer scope segmentation in the paper map, the binding constraint on extraction
recall, and a context gate treating unknown coverage as unknown rather than
disqualifying --- both serving the one property a literature-derived scenario needs,
that the conditions under which an effect holds survive to the answer.
