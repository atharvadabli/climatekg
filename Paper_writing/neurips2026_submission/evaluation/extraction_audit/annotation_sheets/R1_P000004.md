## Complete Study Settings

- [ ] `R1_P000004:P000004_C001` CESM1.2 global climate simulation setup; location: Global land excluding Antarctica
  - `other` / Model version and components: Version 1.2 of the Community Earth System Model (CESM1.2), a fully coupled global climate model, utilizing version 5.3 of the Community Atmosphere Model (CAM5) for atmospheric processes and version 4.0 of the Community Land Model (CLM4) for land surface representation.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Horizontal grid resolution: Simulations were conducted at a horizontal resolution of 0.90° × 1.25°, covering global land areas excluding Antarctica.
  - `climate` / Simulation period and spin-up: All simulations cover the period 1976–2010 (35 years), with the first 5 years considered as spin-up time and excluded from analysis. The analysis period is effectively 1981–2010.
  - `atmosphere` / Initial condition perturbations: On 1 January 1976, small random perturbations on the order of 10^-14 K were imposed on the initial temperature field to generate unique realizations of natural variability within the ensemble members.
  - `hydrology` / Sea surface temperature forcing: Prescribed transient monthly sea surface temperatures and sea ice fractions were used, derived from the data set described by Hurrell et al. [2008], to focus on land-atmospheric interactions without ocean-atmosphere feedbacks.
  - `atmosphere` / Greenhouse gas concentrations: Transient greenhouse gas concentrations were prescribed from measurements rather than being simulated interactively.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation phenology forcing: Satellite-based observations of vegetation phenology were imposed in CLM4 rather than being simulated dynamically.
  - `land_surface` / Land cover static assumption: Static land cover corresponding to the year 2000 was assumed to technically enable switching on the irrigation module, differing from transient land use in other CESM1.0 simulations.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Ensemble design: Two five-member ensembles were generated: a control ensemble (CTL) without irrigation and an identical ensemble (IRR) with the irrigation module activated, to limit effects of natural variability.
- [ ] `R1_P000004:P000004_C002` CTL ensemble without irrigation
  - `other` / Irrigation module status: The control ensemble (CTL) simulations were conducted with the irrigation module switched off, representing a baseline scenario without anthropogenic irrigation water application to crop tiles.
- [ ] `R1_P000004:P000004_C003` IRR ensemble with irrigation module activated
  - `other` / Irrigation module activation: The irrigation module in the Community Land Model (CLM4) is switched on, distinguishing this ensemble from the control experiment where it is deactivated.
  - `land_surface` / Irrigated crop tile separation: Irrigated crops are placed on a separate soil column within grid cells to avoid irrigation water affecting other plant functional types, with irrigated and rainfed fractions subdivided according to high-resolution global data.
  - `hydrology` / Irrigation water source and application: Irrigation water is extracted from total liquid runoff and applied directly to the ground surface of the irrigated crop tile, mimicking extraction from nearby rivers without imposing limitations on water availability.
  - `hydrology` / Target soil moisture replenishment: Soil moisture deficit in irrigated crop tiles is replenished daily to a target content computed at 6 A.M. local time, based on crop water demands and soil saturation, constrained by global observed irrigation amounts.
- [ ] `R1_P000004:P000004_C004` Irrigated crop tiles within grid cells
  - `spatial_configuration` / Separate soil column structure: The irrigated crop tile is placed on a separate soil column within the grid cell, distinct from other plant functional types which share a single soil column. This structural separation enables the soils underneath rainfed and irrigated crop tiles to respond differently to identical atmospheric forcing.
  - `land_surface` / Subgrid-scale surface temperature difference: Irrigated crop tiles are on average 0.81 K colder compared to rainfed crop tiles within the same grid cells in the IRR ensemble, representing the strongest impact on surface temperature recorded at the subgrid scale.
  - `hydrology` / Subgrid-scale evapotranspiration difference: Evapotranspiration over irrigated crop tiles is 172 mm yr⁻¹ higher compared to rainfed crop tiles within the same grid cells, a much larger difference than the 16 mm yr⁻¹ observed at the aggregated grid cell level.
- [ ] `R1_P000004:P000004_C005` Rainfed crop tiles within grid cells
  - `spatial_configuration` / Shared soil column with other PFTs: Rainfed crop tiles share a single soil column with other plant functional types within the grid cell, unlike irrigated crops which have a separate soil column. This configuration means rainfed crops compete for water with other vegetation types.
  - `atmosphere` / Identical atmospheric forcing to irrigated tiles: Rainfed crop tiles receive the same atmospheric forcing as irrigated crop tiles within the same grid cell, meaning contributions from changing incoming shortwave and longwave radiation are absent when comparing subgrid-scale effects between the two tile types.
  - `atmosphere` / Modified atmospheric forcing due to irrigation: The rainfed crop tile undergoes an atmospheric forcing that already incorporates the prior effects of irrigation from neighboring irrigated tiles, which generally results in a lower surface temperature than if no irrigation were present.
