## Complete Study Settings

- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C001` GEMRAMS regional climate model simulation over southern South America; location: southern South America domain
  - `spatial_configuration` / simulation domain geometry: The model domain covers southern South America, spanning latitudes from 8°N to 45°S and longitudes from 35°W to 90°W. The spatial resolution is approximate.
  - `atmosphere` / lateral boundary conditions source: Atmospheric lateral boundary conditions and initial atmospheric fields were provided by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts 40-year reanalysis (ERA-40).
  - `atmosphere` / internal nudging timescale: A 24-hour timescale was applied for internal nudging of the atmospheric model.
  - `hydrology` / sea surface temperature forcing: Climatological sea surface temperatures from the NCEP global one-degree grid database were used, updated on a daily basis.
  - `climate` / simulation temporal coverage: Simulations covered a 5-month period from September to January (early spring to mid-summer) for three distinct years: 1996–1997, 1997–1998, and 1999–2000.
  - `substrate_terrain` / initial soil moisture profile structure: Vertically uniform soil moisture content was assumed throughout the soil profile for all standard simulations.
  - `substrate_terrain` / initial soil moisture data source: Soil water content was initialized using the Soil Water Index (SWI) derived from European Remote Sensing Satellites scatterometer, averaged for 1992–2000 and combined with wilting point, field capacity, and porosity values.
  - `land_surface` / initial vegetation state data source: Initial leaf area index and vegetation fraction were estimated using 8 km × 8 km bimonthly GIMMS-NDVI satellite data for September of the respective start years.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C002` CTRL experiment with current agricultural landscape
  - `land_surface` / current agricultural land cover configuration: The control experiment utilized a current vegetation description representing the contemporary agricultural landscape. This included specific crop types such as wheat, soybean, and corn distributed across the domain, replacing natural vegetation like tall grass, wooded grasslands, or evergreen broadleaf forest in various regions.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C003` NAT experiment with natural pre-settlement vegetation
  - `land_surface` / natural pre-settlement vegetation cover: The NAT experiment represents conditions before European settlement. Croplands were replaced by tall grass, wooded grasslands, or evergreen broadleaf forest depending on geographical location, as depicted in Figure 1b and Table I.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C004` AFFOR experiment with potential future afforestation scenario
  - `land_surface` / afforestation land cover modification: Several grid cells representing current vegetation were replaced by evergreen broadleaf trees to simulate a potential future afforestation scenario. The specific areas for conversion were based on studies by Nosetto et al. (2005) and SAGYPA (2000). Current vegetation cover was assumed in the rest of the model domain.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C005` Sensitivity runs with homogeneous initial soil moisture conditions
  - `substrate_terrain` / initial soil moisture saturation level: The sensitivity runs were initialized with a spatially homogeneous volumetric soil moisture content corresponding to 50% of saturation.
  - `substrate_terrain` / initial soil moisture spatial distribution: The initial soil moisture conditions were horizontally and vertically homogeneous across the simulation domain for these specific sensitivity experiments.
  - `climate` / sensitivity run temporal coverage: The sensitivity simulations with homogeneous initial soil moisture were conducted specifically for the 1996–1997 period.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C006` ENSO classification simulation periods
  - `climate` / ENSO classification basis for period selection: The simulation periods were selected based on their El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) classification to isolate effects under distinct large-scale atmospheric conditions, including non-ENSO, strong El Niño, and La Niña drought phases.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C007` CTRLh experiment with homogeneous initial soil moisture
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C008` NATh experiment with homogeneous initial soil moisture
  - `land_surface` / vegetation cover type: The experiment utilizes natural pre-settlement vegetation conditions, distinguishing it from the control experiment which uses current agricultural landscape descriptions.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C009` Non-ENSO period September 1996 to January 1997
  - `climate` / ENSO classification status: The simulation period from September 1996 to January 1997 is explicitly classified as a non-ENSO event, distinguishing it from the strong El Niño and La Niña periods also simulated in the study.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C010` Strong El Niño period September 1997 to January 1998
  - `climate` / ENSO event classification: The period is classified as a strong El Niño event, considered one of the most important El Niño occurrences on record.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C011` La Niña drought period September 1999 to January 2000
  - `climate` / La Niña drought characterization: The period was characterized by drought conditions from September 1999 to February 2000 in northeastern Argentina, resulting in crop damage and production losses in most of the region.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C012` Agricultural shift from C3 grasslands to crops
  - `land_surface` / vegetation type transition: The land cover change scenario involves a shift from C3 grasses to crops. This specific conversion is distinguished from other agricultural shifts, such as replacing C4 grasses, wooded grasslands, or trees.
  - `land_surface` / leaf area index change: The shift from C3 grasses to crops is associated with an increase in leaf area index (LAI). This structural change drives the observed modifications in energy partitioning.
  - `atmosphere` / surface energy flux response: The conversion leads to a decrease in sensible heat (SH) fluxes and an increase in latent heat (LH) fluxes. This change in partitioning is reflected in the Bowen ratio values.
  - `atmosphere` / near-surface temperature response: The shift from C3 grasses to crops results in a cooling effect on the near-surface atmosphere. This contrasts with warming effects observed when crops replace other vegetation types.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C013` Agricultural shift from C4 grasses or trees to crops
  - `land_surface` / vegetation type replaced by crops: The agricultural shift involved replacing C4 grasses, wooded grasslands, or trees with crops. This specific land cover change is distinguished from shifts involving C3 grasses.
  - `atmosphere` / near-surface temperature response: The replacement of C4 grasses, wooded grasslands, or trees by crops resulted in a warming effect on the near-surface atmosphere.
  - `atmosphere` / surface energy flux partitioning: The shift led to a decrease in latent heat (LH) fluxes, associated with a decrease in leaf area index (LAI), and a small increase in sensible heat (SH) fluxes.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C014` Southern and central Pampas agricultural shift
  - `atmosphere` / diurnal temperature range response: The shift to agricultural scenarios resulted in a decrease in the diurnal temperature range (DTR) over most of the domain, particularly in the Pampas. This decrease was especially pronounced during spring due to a larger reduction in maximum temperatures compared to minimum temperatures.
  - `atmosphere` / seasonal temperature difference mechanisms: For the wheat-tall grass conversion, the decrease in DTR was attributed to a larger decrease of maximum temperatures in spring. In summer, the decrease in DTR was due to a larger increase of minimum temperatures. For soybean-C3 grassland conversions, the DTR decrease was mostly due to lower maximum temperatures under current vegetation conditions.
  - `atmosphere` / summer nighttime and daytime temperature coupling: In the southern part of the Pampas during summer, slight nighttime warming coincided with slight daytime cooling. This extra nighttime warming was supplied by a slight increase in sensible heat flux (SH) during the night under current vegetation conditions.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C015` Northern domain agricultural shift
  - `climate` / diurnal temperature range response: In the northern part of the domain, the diurnal temperature range (DTR) shows an increase under current vegetation conditions. This increase is mainly due to a larger increase of maximum temperatures than of minimum temperatures.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C016` Grid cells converting from wheat/soybean to C3 grasses
  - `land_surface` / vegetation conversion type: Grid cells where the land cover shifted from wheat and soybean crops to C3 grasses, representing a reversal of agricultural expansion into natural grasslands.
  - `atmosphere` / latent heat flux response: Latent heat flux values were higher over the crops (wheat/soybean) compared to the C3 grasslands in these converted areas, indicating a shift in energy partitioning toward latent heat due to increased leaf area index.
  - `atmosphere` / sensible heat flux response: Sensible heat flux showed a decrease in the current vegetation (crops) relative to the natural C3 grasslands, consistent with the opposite pattern of latent heat changes and increased albedo reducing available energy.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C017` Grid cells converting from trees/wooded grasslands to crops
  - `land_surface` / vegetation conversion type: Grid cells where natural vegetation, specifically trees and wooded grasslands, was converted to crops. This conversion is associated with lower leaf area index (LAI) and rooting depth in the current agricultural vegetation compared to the natural state.
  - `atmosphere` / sensible heat flux response: In areas where trees and wooded grasslands were converted to crops, sensible heat (SH) showed a decrease. This reduction in SH is attributed to the lower amount of available energy combined with lower LAI and rooting depth in the current crop vegetation.
  - `atmosphere` / latent heat flux response: In areas where trees and wooded grasslands were converted to crops, average latent heat (LH) was lower in the current vegetation (crops) than in natural vegetation. This decrease is linked to lower available energy, lower LAI, and reduced rooting depth.
  - `atmosphere` / Bowen ratio change: The Bowen ratio (beta), representing the partitioning of available energy between sensible and latent heat, showed a largest positive relative change of 26% in spring for grid cells that shifted from wooded grasslands to soybean.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C018` El Nino 1997-1998 shortgrass/soybean conversion cells
  - `land_surface` / vegetation conversion type: The grid cells are characterized by a land-use change shifting from shortgrass vegetation, and in some specific grid cells from soybean crops, to the conditions simulated in the AFFOR scenario.
  - `atmosphere` / sensible heat flux difference response: The sensible heat (SH) flux differences reversed direction in these cells during the 1997-1998 period, averaging 24 W m⁻², compared to -97 W m⁻² during the 1999-2000 period.
  - `atmosphere` / latent heat flux difference response: Latent heat (LH) flux differences were small but remained positive during the 1997-1998 period in these grid cells, resulting from weak responses due to similar canopy conductance between scenarios.
  - `land_surface` / canopy conductance comparison: The model simulated similar and slightly lower canopy conductance in the shortgrass and soybean grid cells under the CTRL scenario compared to the AFFOR scenario.
- [ ] `R1_P000015:P000015_C019` El Nino 1997-1998 shortgrass/soybean temperature reversal
  - `atmosphere` / temperature difference sign reversal: In grid cells converting from shortgrass or soybean, the AFFOR–CTRL temperature difference signal reversed direction during the 1997–1998 period. While generally negative elsewhere, differences in these specific cells were positive, reaching up to 0.1°C.
