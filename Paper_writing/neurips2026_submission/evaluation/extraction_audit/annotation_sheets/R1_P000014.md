## Complete Study Settings

- [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C001` Western European temperate forests, MSG satellite observations 2004–2013, daytime JJA focus; location: Western Europe (Landes and Sologne)
  - `spatial_configuration` / Regional spatial extent and resolution: The study focuses on the largest temperate forests in Europe without pronounced orography, specifically Landes (forest area >12,000 km²) and Sologne. Analysis uses high-resolution spatial data down to 1 km and high-frequency temporal observations at 15-minute intervals from geostationary MSG satellites.
  - `substrate_terrain` / Topographic flatness and elevation range: Orography is nearly absent in both regions to exclude confounding topographic lifting effects. Maximum gradual elevation differences are in the order of 100–200 m over the region, allowing isolation of regional-scale land-use effects from orographic influences.
  - `land_surface` / Forest cover and species composition: Landes is a planted maritime pine (Pinus pinaster) forest, while Sologne consists of original broadleaf species dominated by oak (Quercus petraea and Quercus robur). Both regions feature sharp forest–cropland contrasts in several directions, with forests surrounded by crop and grassland.
  - `climate` / Summer temperature and precipitation climatology: Landes and Sologne experience similar climate conditions. June–August (JJA) temperatures are 19.3°C for Sologne and 20.9°C for Landes. JJA precipitation is 150 mm for Sologne and 143 mm for Landes.
  - `atmosphere` / Observation time window and season: Analysis focuses on daytime hours (6–18 UTC) during the summer season (June–August, JJA). This period was selected to evaluate cloud frequency differences between forest and non-forest areas under conditions where thermal activity is significant.
  - `atmosphere` / Cloud detection methodology and thresholds: Two independent cloud detection algorithms were used: MSG-CPP (using multiple channels, cloud optical thickness >0.3) and MSG-HRV (using highest resolution visible information). Cloud frequency is evaluated as the fraction of time clouds are detected within a pixel.
  - `atmosphere` / Wind speed data source: Regional wind conditions were assessed using 10 m wind speed data obtained from ERA-Interim reanalysis. This data was used to analyze the impact of regional meridional wind components on cloud frequency.
- [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C002` Landes forest region, France; location: Landes
  - `land_surface` / Forest species composition: The Landes region consists of a planted maritime pine (Pinus pinaster) forest. This contrasts with the broadleaf oak species found in the Sologne region, representing a distinct land cover type within the study area.
  - `climate` / Summer temperature and precipitation values: The region experiences June–August (JJA) temperatures of 20.9°C and JJA precipitation of 143 mm. These climatic conditions are similar to those in the Sologne region, allowing for comparative analysis.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Forest area extent: The Landes forest covers an area of over 12,000 km². This large spatial extent is sufficient for atmospheric boundary layer conditions to reflect the specific surface conditions of the forest.
  - `land_surface` / Surrounding land cover contrast: The large forest area is surrounded by crop and grassland, creating sharp forest–cropland contrasts in several directions. This configuration facilitates the isolation of regional-scale land-use effects.
- [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C003` Sologne forest region, France; location: Sologne
  - `land_surface` / Forest species composition and origin: The Sologne region consists of original broadleaf tree cover, dominated by oak species (*Quercus petraea* and *Quercus robur*). This contrasts with the planted maritime pine forest in the Landes region.
  - `climate` / Summer temperature and precipitation values: Sologne experiences June–August (JJA) temperatures of 19.3°C and JJA precipitation of 150 mm. These conditions are similar to those in the Landes region.
  - `atmosphere` / Seasonal switch in cloud preference: A seasonal switch in cloud preference exists for Sologne. During JJA, there is a significant preference for clouds to occur over the forest, whereas outside this period, differences in cloud frequency are not significant.
  - `atmosphere` / North-south gradient in cloud cover: A strong north–south gradient in cloud cover is present for Sologne, indicating that local land-use effects are superimposed on larger-scale weather patterns.
  - `atmosphere` / Lack of wind-dependent edge effects: No clear relation between patterns of cloud frequency and regional wind conditions was found for the smaller Sologne forest, where edge effects are more difficult to detect compared to larger forests.
  - `land_surface` / Land surface temperature dynamics: Sologne exhibits a strong increase in land surface temperature (up to 10 K) over surrounding areas during summer, indicative of declining evaporation rates, while forest LST remains stable due to sustained evapotranspiration.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation index seasonal dynamics: In Sologne, the Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) is initially higher over surrounding non-forest areas but decreases strongly during summer due to harvesting, leading to strong surface warming.

  - `land_surface` / Forest cover spatial pattern: The Sologne forest area is surrounded by crop and grassland, creating sharp forest–cropland contrasts in several directions. - [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C007` Non-forest agricultural surroundings in Sologne
  - `land_surface` / Surrounding land cover type: The non-forest areas surrounding the Sologne forest consist of crop and grassland, creating sharp forest–cropland contrasts in several directions typical for the western European landscape.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation index seasonal dynamics in surroundings: In the Sologne surroundings, the Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) is initially higher than over the forest but decreases strongly during summer due to harvesting activities.

- [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C008` Pre-cyclone Klaus period (2004–2008) for Landes forest
  - `atmosphere` / Pre-cyclone cloud frequency seasonal amplitude: Before the 2009 cyclone Klaus, the difference in cloud frequency between forest and non-forest areas exhibited a marked seasonal cycle peaking in summer. The amplitude of this cycle was 0.114 (95% confidence interval: 0.093–0.135), indicating strong thermal activity and moistening effects over the intact forest during the 2004–2008 period.
- [ ] `R1_P000014:P000014_C009` Post-cyclone Klaus period (2009–2013) for Landes forest
  - `atmosphere` / Post-cyclone cloud frequency reduction: In the 5-year period following cyclone Klaus (2009–2013), local changes in JJA cloud frequency reached up to –0.20 compared to the pre-cyclone period (2004–2008). This decrease was statistically significant (P=0.001) over the forest box with maximum windthrow, whereas no significant changes were found outside the storm-affected area.
  - `atmosphere` / Seasonal amplitude of cloud difference attenuation: The seasonal cycle of the cloud frequency difference between forest and non-forest areas, which previously peaked in summer with an amplitude of 0.114 (95% confidence: 0.093–0.135), reduced by almost an order of magnitude to 0.020 (95% confidence: 0.002–0.038) after the cyclone event.
