- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C001` Odisha surface temperature and LULC study framework (1981–2010); location: Odisha, Eastern India
  - `spatial_configuration` / Study region location and extent: The state of Odisha in Eastern India, extending approximately from 81°E to 88°E and 17°N to 23°N. The region is surrounded by the Bay of Bengal to the East and the Indian peninsula to the west.
  - `climate` / Climatological temperature and rainfall regime: The region has a tropical climate with high surface temperatures during April and May, often leading to heat waves. Most of the state has an annual mean temperature greater than 26 °C. Annual average rainfall is approximately 1500 mm, primarily from the south-west monsoon during June to September.
  - `atmosphere` / Atmospheric circulation influences: The region is influenced by monsoon depressions and tropical cyclones that make landfall from the Bay of Bengal during both pre-monsoon and post-monsoon seasons.
  - `substrate_terrain` / Topographic variation and altitude zones: The study area includes areas lying 500 m above mean sea level (amsl) and lower altitude plains. Lower temperatures are observed over high-altitude locations compared to the rest of the state.
  - `land_surface` / Land use and land cover characteristics: The landscape features heterogeneous land use including urban/built-up areas, various crop types (Kharif, Rabi, Zaid, Double), fallow lands, grasslands, plantations, evergreen and deciduous forests, shrubland, swamps, wastelands, gullies, scrubland, water bodies, snow, and shifting cultivation.
  - `other` / Study period and temporal scope: The analysis covers the period from 1981 to 2010, utilizing long-term ground, satellite, and reanalysis datasets to investigate surface temperature changes and their relation to land use and land cover.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C002` Decadal temperature trend analysis
  - `climate` / Overall decadal warming magnitude: The state of Odisha underwent a mean surface temperature warming of approximately 0.3 °C during the period 1981 to 2010. This positive trend was observed irrespective of meteorological station location (coastal or non-coastal) and altitude, with statistical significance at the 95% confidence level in most cases.
  - `climate` / Inter-decadal temperature variability pattern: Temperature trends exhibited large inter-decadal variability. The first decade (1981–1990) showed a mean temperature decrease of approximately -0.7 °C for sites below 500 m amsl. In contrast, the subsequent decades (1991–2000 and 2001–2010) showed increases of approximately 1 °C and 0.8 °C respectively.
  - `climate` / Diurnal temperature range trends: The diurnal temperature range (DTR) increased by approximately 1.16 °C during the 1980s. However, it decreased by approximately -0.46 °C in the second decade (1991–2000) and by approximately -1.19 °C in the third decade (2001–2010) for areas below 500 m amsl.
  - `climate` / Minimum vs maximum temperature trends: In the recent two decades, both minimum and maximum temperatures increased. Minimum temperatures rose by approximately 1.2 °C, while maximum temperatures rose by approximately 0.13 °C. This differential increase contributed to the reduction in diurnal temperature range.
  - `climate` / Seasonal temperature trend characteristics: Between 1981 and 1990, temperatures reduced by 0.3 to 0.9 °C depending on the season. Since 1991, the region warmed by approximately 0.4 to 0.9 °C. The largest rate of increase was observed in June–August (JJA) and September–November (SON), while the least was in December–February (DJF).
  - `other` / ENSO event exclusion criteria: To assess the impact of El-Nino/Southern Oscillation (ENSO) events, years with extreme ENSO events (1982, 1983, 1997, and 1998) were removed from the analysis. Results with and without these ENSO years were found to be consistent.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C003` Low-altitude station analysis (<500 m amsl)
  - `substrate_terrain` / Altitude threshold for station selection: The analysis focuses specifically on meteorological stations located below 500 meters above mean sea level (amsl), distinguishing these low-altitude plains sites from higher altitude locations within the study region.
  - `climate` / Decadal temperature trend variability: Mean temperature for sites below 500 m amsl decreased by approximately -0.7°C during 1981–1990, but increased by ~1°C during 1991–2000 and ~0.8°C during 2001–2010. Minimum temperatures showed clear increases in the recent two decades.
  - `climate` / Diurnal temperature range trends: The diurnal temperature range (DTR) increased by ~1.16°C during the 1980s but decreased by ~-0.46°C in 1991–2000 and ~-1.19°C in 2001–2010 for areas below 500 m amsl.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C004` LULC classification and NDVI trends (2004–2010)
  - `land_surface` / LULC classification dataset and resolution: Land use and land cover classes were derived from Advanced Wide Field Sensor (AWiFS) datasets on the Resourcesat-1 satellite. The analysis utilized a gridded product with 1 km spatial resolution for the years 2004 and 2010 to quantify changes in categories such as crops, fallow lands, and vegetation.
  - `land_surface` / NDVI dataset and resolution: Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) trends were analyzed using MODIS-Terra (MOD13C2) data. This dataset provides atmospherically corrected, cloud-free surface reflectance observations with a spatial resolution of 5.6 km x 5.6 km for the period 2001 to 2010.
  - `other` / LULC change analysis period: The specific assessment of land use and land cover changes using AWiFS data covers the period from 2004 to 2010. This timeframe coincides with the recent decade of temperature trend analysis discussed in the study.
  - `land_surface` / Observed LULC class changes: Analysis revealed a ~97% increase in Rabi Crop cultivation (October to March) at the expense of Kharif Crops (July to October). Overall green vegetation decreased, particularly in the eastern part of the state, indicating agricultural diversification and changing cropping patterns.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C005` 1981–1990 decadal temperature trends
  - `climate` / Mean surface temperature trend: During the first decade (1981–1990), the mean surface temperature for sites below 500 m above mean sea level decreased by approximately -0.7 °C, contrasting with warming trends in subsequent decades.
  - `climate` / Diurnal temperature range trend: The diurnal temperature range (DTR) increased by approximately 1.16 °C during the 1980s for areas below 500 m amsl, showing an opposing trend to the decreases observed in later decades.
  - `climate` / Seasonal temperature reduction magnitude: Between 1981 and 1990, temperatures reduced by 0.3 to 0.9 °C depending on the season, a cooling evident on both annual and seasonal scales across the region.
  - `climate` / Observation minus reanalysis trend: The first decade since 1981 displayed a declining trend in Observation Minus Reanalysis (OMR) of approximately -0.04 °C/year over the whole state, indicating no significant LULC-induced warming signal.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C006` 1991–2000 decadal temperature trends
  - `climate` / Mean surface temperature trend: During the decade 1991–2000, the mean surface temperature for sites below 500 m above mean sea level increased by approximately 1 °C. This warming trend contrasts with the cooling observed in the previous decade (1981–1990).
  - `climate` / Diurnal temperature range trend: The diurnal temperature range (DTR) decreased by approximately -0.46 °C during the 1991–2000 decade for areas below 500 m amsl. This reduction contrasts with the DTR increase observed in the 1980s.
  - `climate` / Seasonal temperature warming magnitude: The region warmed by approximately 0.4 to 0.9 °C since 1991. The largest rate of temperature increase was observed during the June–August (JJA) and September–November (SON) periods, while the least increase occurred in December–February (DJF).
  - `climate` / Minimum vs maximum temperature differential: Since 1991, minimum temperatures increased at a much faster rate than maximum temperatures across all seasons. This differential increase contributed to the observed reduction in the diurnal temperature range during this decade.
  - `climate` / OMR trend indicating LULC impact: The Observation Minus Reanalysis (OMR) showed a clear increasing trend over Odisha during 1991–2000, particularly in the western part of the state. This positive OMR indicates warming associated with land use and land cover changes or urbanization.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C007` 2001–2010 decadal temperature trends
  - `climate` / Mean surface temperature trend magnitude: The mean surface temperature increased by approximately 0.8 °C during the decade 2001–2010 for sites below 500 m above mean sea level, representing an accelerated warming phase compared to previous decades.
  - `climate` / Diurnal temperature range trend: The diurnal temperature range decreased by approximately -1.19 °C during the 2001–2010 decade for areas below 500 m amsl, contrasting with the increase observed in the 1980s.
  - `climate` / Minimum and maximum temperature differential trends: Both minimum and maximum temperatures increased, with minimum temperatures rising by approximately 1.2 °C and maximum temperatures by approximately 0.13 °C, contributing to the reduction in diurnal temperature range.
  - `climate` / OMR trend spatial pattern and magnitude: The Observation minus Reanalysis (OMR) trend showed a clear increasing pattern, with the highest increasing trend of approximately 0.04 °C/year coinciding with densely populated cities such as Bhubaneswar and Cuttack in the eastern part of the state.
  - `land_surface` / LULC-induced warming contribution: Approximately 25% to 50% of the observed overall warming during 2001–2010 is associated with land use and land cover changes, with urban centers like Bhubaneswar and Cuttack experiencing up to 40-50% contribution from LULC.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation cover change pattern: There was a general decreasing trend in Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) over the eastern part of the state during 2001–2010, associated with agricultural diversification and changes in cropping patterns.
  - `atmosphere` / Surface heat flux changes: Latent Heat Flux (LHF) decreased and Sensible Heat Flux (SHF) increased over the eastern part of Odisha during 2001–2010, favoring warming and consistent with LULC-induced temperature changes.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C008` Urban center warming quantification (Bhubaneswar, Cuttack); location: Bhubaneswar and Cuttack
  - `climate` / LULC-induced warming contribution percentage: Urban centers Bhubaneswar and Cuttack experienced temperature increases of approximately 40% and 50% respectively during 2001–2010 attributable to land use and land cover changes, representing the highest fraction of total warming among district headquarters.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Urban center identification and population status: Bhubaneswar and Cuttack are identified as the most populous cities in Odisha and district headquarters. Bhubaneswar is characterized as a fast-growing tier 2 city, serving as key locations for quantifying urbanization signatures.
- [ ] `R1_P000020:P000020_C009` Surface heat flux analysis (LHF, SHF); location: Eastern Odisha
  - `atmosphere` / Latent and sensible heat flux trends: Latent Heat Flux (LHF) decreased and Sensible Heat Flux (SHF) increased over the eastern part of Odisha during 2001–2010. These changes favor warming and are consistent with land use and land cover-induced temperature changes.
  - `other` / Heat flux data source and resolution: Sensible and latent heat flux datasets were obtained from the NCEP Climate Forecast System Version 2 (CFS v2). The product has a spatial resolution of 30 km × 30 km.
