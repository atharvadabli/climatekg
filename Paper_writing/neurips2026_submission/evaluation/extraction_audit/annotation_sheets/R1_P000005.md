  - `spatial_configuration` / Flight track geometry and extent: A north-south flight track covering a distance of 325 km, extending from Niger into the Republic of Benin.
  - `substrate_terrain` / Topographic relief along flight path: Relatively flat topography varying from 200 to 400 m above sea level, with maximum variations of 50 m in the region between 10.7°N and 12°N.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation heterogeneity and land cover types: Contrast between mesoscale forest features associated with Parc W (protected natural vegetation) and extensive surrounding fields. Land surface dominated by shrubland, cropland, and forest, with sharp boundaries between shrub and cropland.
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind conditions: Quiet synoptic conditions with only weak northwesterlies across the Republic of Benin. Light-wind conditions expected to enhance local or mesoscale effects.
  - `atmosphere` / Cloud types and convective activity: Intermittent cloud presence along the flight trajectory, composed primarily of cumulus congestus, with some cumulonimbus and precipitation. Deep convection initiated a few kilometers west of the flight path during and after aircraft runs.
  - `climate` / Time of day for observations: Observations took place in the early afternoon, when the atmospheric response to the land surface would be maximized, but the rate of change of planetary boundary layer properties was relatively slow.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C002` 17 August 2006 aircraft case study over flat topography; location: 10.7°N to 12°N along flight track
  - `spatial_configuration` / Aircraft flight legs and altitudes: Data collected from two specific legs: a southward leg through cumulus congestus cloud layer at a mean altitude of 1150 m above ground level (1253-1340 UTC), and a return leg at a lower mean altitude of 190 m above ground level (1346-1431 UTC).
  - `spatial_configuration` / Analysis domain exclusion due to downdraft: The region between 10.3°N and 10.7°N along the flight path is excluded from analysis because it displays characteristics of a downdraft (reduced PBL temperatures, reduced isoprene, peak ozone) caused by developing convection, meaning PBL properties there are not closely related to the land surface.
  - `atmosphere` / Mesoscale circulation and convergence zones: Persistent mesoscale forcing of winds attributed to land surface impact, with areas of convergence and divergence coinciding with cloud presence between 10.7°N and 12.0°N. Specific low-level convergence zones identified at 11°N (with divergence aloft) and 11.8°N (with sloping front evidence).
  - `atmosphere` / Temperature-isoprene relationship and boundary layer depth: Negative relationship between temperature anomalies and isoprene concentrations at wavelengths larger than 14 km. Higher evapotranspiration over shrubland leads to increased latent heat fluxes, decreased sensible heat fluxes, and cool PBL temperature anomalies, potentially increasing boundary layer depth by up to 42%.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C003` MSG satellite seasonal cloud climatology for June-August; location: Benin-Niger flight track
  - `atmosphere` / Seasonal cloud cover climatology: A seasonal climatology of shallow cloud cover derived from MSG satellite channel 1 visible data (0.56–0.71 μm). The logarithmic mean was calculated for June, July, and August of 2006 and 2007 along the flight path to reduce the impact of highly reflective events on the mean.
  - `atmosphere` / Diurnal cloud asymmetry analysis: Analysis of net cloud impact by subtracting morning data (time t before local noon) from afternoon data (time t after local noon). Positive values denote enhanced afternoon cloud cover, while negative values denote enhanced morning cloud cover, isolating the diurnal cycle along the flight path.
  - `climate` / Temporal window of climatology: The seasonal climatology covers the months of June, July, and August for the years 2006 and 2007, representing the monsoon season period used to assess climatological significance beyond the single case study.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C004` Southward cloud-level transect at 1150 m altitude; location: southward flight segment
  - `atmosphere` / Cloud layer composition and updrafts: The transect passed through a cumulus congestus cloud layer. Liquid water content and isoprene concentrations showed coincident peaks, indicating that chemical species were transported exclusively by small-scale convective updrafts in the absence of synoptically forced large-scale uplift.
  - `atmosphere` / Isoprene concentration variability in clouds: Cloud-level isoprene concentrations varied significantly, ranging from levels close to planetary boundary layer concentrations (e.g., at 11.8°N) to considerably lower values (e.g., at 11°N), likely depending on updraft strength and residence time within the clouds.
  - `atmosphere` / Meridional wind flow in cloud layer: A mean northerly flow was observed in the cloud layer. The ratio between horizontal and vertical velocities in the lower level was 10, consistent with the presence of a sloping front, although some propagation between observation times is also possible.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C005` Return low-level transect at 190 m altitude; location: return flight segment
  - `spatial_configuration` / Flight leg timing and altitude: The return leg was flown at a lower mean altitude of 190 m above ground level, occurring between 1346 and 1431 UTC.
  - `atmosphere` / Low-level convergence zones: Two main areas of low-level convergence were identified along the transect. At 11°N, divergence in meridional winds was observed in the cloud layer directly above. At 11.8°N, evidence suggested a sloping front with divergence aloft displaced southward by approximately 10 km relative to the convergence zone below.
  - `atmosphere` / Isoprene and temperature relationship at low level: A negative relationship between virtual potential temperature anomalies and isoprene concentrations was observed at 190 m altitude. This relationship was significant at the 95% level for wavelengths larger than 14 km, attributed to deeper boundary layer mixing over warmer surfaces diluting isoprene.
  - `atmosphere` / Meridional wind and temperature gradient correlation: Aircraft data at 190 m showed correlations between meridional wind velocity and virtual potential temperature gradients. The circulation affected the whole planetary boundary layer, with horizontal to vertical velocity ratios of 10 consistent with a sloping front.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C006` Cropland regions along the aircraft flight track; location: cropland areas along track
  - `land_surface` / Cropland land cover classification: Regions identified as cropland using the GlobCover Land Cover satellite remote sensing product, which differentiates between forest/shrubland and cropland based on UN Land Cover Classification System categories.
  - `climate` / Enhanced afternoon cloud cover over cropland: Seasonal climatology of visible satellite data indicates enhanced cloud cover in the afternoon specifically over cropland regions, suggesting climatological significance of the observed dynamics.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C007` Forest and shrubland regions along the aircraft flight track; location: forest/shrubland areas along track
  - `land_surface` / Forest and shrubland cover fraction: The fraction of forest or shrub cover along the flight path is estimated by averaging the percentage per category over a length scale of 5 km, differentiating these regions from surrounding cropland.
  - `land_surface` / Vegetation heterogeneity scale and boundaries: Land surface variations are dominated by shrubland, cropland, and forest. Heterogeneities range from kilometers to several tens of kilometers in size, with apparent sharp boundaries between shrub and cropland.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C008` Afternoon cloud cover analysis relative to local noon; location: Benin-Niger flight track
  - `atmosphere` / Afternoon cloud cover enhancement: A bias toward more cloud in the mid-afternoon (1.5–3.0 hours after local noon) between 11.1°N and 11.8°N, with peaks at 11.4°N and 11.55°N. This enhancement is consistent with increased cloud cover over cropland regions.
  - `atmosphere` / Early afternoon cloud initiation at boundaries: At 1 hour after local noon, smaller peaks in cloud cover appear at 11.2°N and 11.6°N, suggesting the initiation of clouds along the edges of cropland boundaries before developing into widespread signals later in the day.
- [ ] `R1_P000005:P000005_C009` Morning cloud cover analysis relative to local noon; location: Benin-Niger flight track
  - `atmosphere` / Morning cloud cover anomaly definition: Negative values in the diurnal asymmetry analysis denote enhanced morning cloud cover. This is derived by subtracting data at time t before local noon from data at time t after local noon, isolating the net impact of clouds relative to the afternoon.
  - `atmosphere` / Morning reflectivity bias: In the absence of cloud, measured reflectivity is expected to be higher in the morning than in the afternoon due to Sun and viewing angle dependencies. This leads to a predominance of negative values in the analysis, particularly when comparing dusk and dawn.
