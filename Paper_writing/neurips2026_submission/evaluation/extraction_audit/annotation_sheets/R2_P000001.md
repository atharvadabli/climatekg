Patches alternate between 'WET' (greater latent heat flux) and 'DRY' (lesser latent heat flux). The sum of latent plus sensible heat fluxes is constant throughout the domain.
  - `land_surface` / Surface heat flux contrast magnitude: The contrast of surface heat fluxes between patches is maintained at 60% of the domain-averaged sensible heat flux. For WET patches, 30% of domain-mean sensible heat flux is subtracted from sensible and added to latent; for DRY patches, the reverse applies.
  - `land_surface` / Evaporative fraction at noon: The evaporative fraction for DRY and WET patches at noon local standard time is about 0.75 and 0.45, respectively, compared to 0.6 for the CASS case.
  - `atmosphere` / Background wind configuration: Initial horizontal winds are set to be purely zonal and vertically constant. The wind field is relaxed to its initial state with a 1-h time-scale at all levels.
  - `climate` / Diurnal cycle and case basis: The setup follows the CASS composite case based on summertime observations at the ARM Southern Great Plains site, representing a typical daytime evolution from sunrise to sunset with fair weather nonprecipitating surface-forced shallow cumulus.

## Scientific Claims

- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL001` surface heat flux heterogeneity (chessboard pattern) --causal--> planetary boundary layer updraft intensity (increase): Prescribed chessboard-patterned surface heat flux heterogeneity triggers secondary circulations that intensify PBL thermals and produce stronger updrafts compared to simulations with uniform domain-mean surface heat fluxes. Evidence: R2_P000001:P000001:S09:P0072
- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL002` surface heat flux heterogeneity (large patch size) --causal--> convective cloud development (transition to deep convection): Chessboard-patterned surface heat flux heterogeneity with patch sizes larger than 5 km triggers a transition from shallow cumulus to deep precipitating convection over DRY patches, whereas homogeneous domain-mean fluxes maintain uniformly distributed shallow clouds. Evidence: R2_P000001:P000001:S06:P0033
- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL003` surface heat flux heterogeneity (inducing secondary circulation) --causal--> boundary layer thermodynamic profiles (modified temperature and humidity): Secondary circulations induced by heterogeneous surface heat fluxes modify PBL thermodynamics, making the mixed layer over DRY patches cooler and moister, and over WET patches warmer and drier, compared to homogeneous simulations with equivalent local surface fluxes. Evidence: R2_P000001:P000001:S10:P0088
- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL004` surface heat flux heterogeneity (driving cross-patch circulation) --causal--> near-surface temperature contrast (decrease): Active cross-patch secondary circulations driven by heterogeneous surface heat fluxes horizontally mix the boundary layer, significantly reducing the near-surface temperature contrast between DRY and WET patches compared to simulations without horizontal mixing. Evidence: R2_P000001:P000001:S11:P0112
- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL005` mesoscale secondary circulation (development) --causal--> vertical moisture transport mechanism (mesoscale dominance over turbulence): In transition cases, vertical moisture transport is primarily driven by mesoscale secondary circulation processes, whereas nontransition cases rely more on turbulence-scale mixing despite similar domain-averaged fluxes. Evidence: R2_P000001:P000001:S09:P0061, R2_P000001:P000001:S09:P0063
- [ ] `R2_P000001:P000001_CL006` mesoscale secondary circulation (strengthening) --causal--> vertical wind variance in planetary boundary layer (decrease): A well-defined secondary circulation suppresses turbulence intensity and reduces vertical wind variance in the planetary boundary layer, particularly over WET patches where broad downdrafts prevail.
