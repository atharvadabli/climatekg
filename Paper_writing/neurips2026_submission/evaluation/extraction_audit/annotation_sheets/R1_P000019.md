## Complete Study Settings

- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C001` MM5V2 model simulations over Rondônia inner domain; location: Rondônia, Amazonia
  - `spatial_configuration` / Nested domain geometry and resolution: Two nested domains were used. The outer domain covers an area of 480 km x 480 km with a grid size of 12 km. The inner domain, located at the center of the outer domain, covers part of Rondônia with dimensions of 240 km x 240 km and a grid size of 4 km.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Vertical atmospheric discretization: The atmosphere up to 100 hPa is divided into 24 sigma levels with specific values: 1.00, 0.99, 0.98, 0.96, 0.93, 0.89, 0.85, 0.80, 0.75, 0.70, 0.65, 0.60, 0.55, 0.50, 0.45, 0.40, 0.35, 0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05, and 0.00.
  - `other` / Numerical model physics and schemes: The MM5V2 model was used with the nonhydrostatic option. Physics include Blackadar PBL parameterization, simple ice microphysics, NCAR Community Climate Model version 2 radiation schemes, shallow convection processes, and Grell convection scheme for the outer domain. No convective parameterization was used for the inner domain.
  - `other` / Simulation temporal setup: All simulations start at 2000 local time and last for 72 hours with a time step of 60 seconds. Initial and boundary conditions were obtained from National Centers for Environmental Prediction reanalysis data.
  - `land_surface` / Land cover data resolution and source: Land cover data representing deforestation percentage from Skole and Tucker (1993) at 16-km resolution was disaggregated onto a 4 km x 4 km gridded mesh over the inner domain of the model.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C002` Break period simulations (3–6 Nov 1990)
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind forcing strength: During the break period (September–November), synoptic systems are relatively weak compared to those in the rainy and dry seasons. This regime allows mesoscale circulations to potentially become active over heterogeneous land surfaces.
  - `climate` / Simulation temporal window: The simulation covers the period from 2000 local time on 3 November to 2000 local time on 6 November 1990. These dates were selected as they fall in the middle of the season, ensuring representative synoptic conditions.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C003` Rainy season simulations (3–6 Jan 1990)
  - `climate` / Rainy season temporal window and regime: The simulation covers the rainy season in Rondônia, extending from December to April. The specific simulation period is from 2000 LT on 3 January 1990 to 2000 LT on 6 January 1990.
  - `atmosphere` / Atmospheric stability and convective parameters: Sounding profiles indicate the atmosphere is conditionally unstable with relatively large CAPE values, small values of CIN, and a low most-unstable level of approximately 970 hPa, which was very close to the surface.
  - `atmosphere` / Dominant convection type and cloud structure: Active deep convection and intensive rainfall were obtained. Deep convection led to high-level clouds above 500 hPa with a peak at around 300 hPa. No low-level or shallow clouds were generated in the rainy-season simulations.
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic forcing strength relative to mesoscale circulation: Mesoscale circulations were inhibited by a relatively strong synoptic forcing during the rainy season. Consequently, land cover variability played no role in shaping the distribution of cloudiness and rainfall.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C004` Dry season simulations (30 Jun–3 Jul 1990)
  - `climate` / Dry season temporal window: The simulation covers the dry season period in Rondônia, specifically from 2000 local time on 30 June to 2000 local time on 3 July 1990. This period represents a climatic regime distinct from the rainy and break periods.
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind variability: The synoptic wind strength varied during the simulation period, being weakest on the first day and strongest on the second day. This variation influenced the development of mesoscale circulations, with stronger winds inhibiting circulation development.
  - `atmosphere` / Convective available potential energy distribution: In the upper-right corner of the domain, covered by uniform forest, a relatively large CAPE of approximately 600 J was present. In contrast, the lower-left corner, also forest-covered, exhibited a much smaller CAPE value of approximately 200 J.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C005` Break period with original synoptic wind and 4-km grid
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind forcing condition: The simulation utilizes the original synoptic wind conditions derived from reanalysis data, as opposed to the reduced synoptic wind experiments where wind magnitude was decreased by a factor of 10.
  - `spatial_configuration` / Horizontal grid resolution: The simulation is conducted using a 4-km horizontal grid resolution for the inner domain, which was validated against 2-km resolution runs to ensure adequate representation of mesoscale processes.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C006` Break period with reduced synoptic wind and 4-km grid
  - `atmosphere` / Reduced synoptic wind forcing magnitude: The experiment used weakened synoptic forcing by reducing the magnitude of the synoptic wind by an arbitrary factor of 10. The large-scale convergence and/or divergence were reduced accordingly to maintain consistency with the modified wind field.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C007` Rainy season with original synoptic wind and 4-km grid
  - `spatial_configuration` / Horizontal grid resolution: The simulations for the rainy season were conducted using a horizontal grid resolution of 4 km. This resolution was chosen due to limited computing resources, following validation that 4-km resolution captures essential aspects of land-atmosphere interaction consistent with 2-km results.
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind forcing magnitude: The simulation utilized original synoptic wind conditions, characterized as relatively strong synoptic forcing. This strong forcing inhibited mesoscale circulations induced by land surface heterogeneity, rendering the impact of deforestation on cloudiness and rainfall distribution negligible.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C008` Rainy season with reduced synoptic wind and 4-km grid
  - `atmosphere` / Reduced synoptic wind forcing: The initial and boundary values of wind velocities were reduced by a factor of 10 compared to the original synoptic conditions, with all other atmospheric conditions remaining unchanged.
  - `atmosphere` / Reduced spatial coverage of afternoon cloudiness: Under the weakened synoptic wind condition, the spatial coverage of afternoon cloudiness was greatly reduced compared to the original simulation. The clouds also occurred at different locations.
  - `atmosphere` / High-level deep convection structure: Deep convection led to high-level clouds above 500 hPa with a peak at around 300 hPa. No low-level clouds were generated in this simulation setting.
  - `hydrology` / Shrunk rainfall area with isolated cells: The area receiving rainfall shrank considerably under the reduced wind condition. Only isolated rainfall cells were simulated, contrasting with the widespread rainfall in the original rainy season simulation.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C009` Dry season with original synoptic wind and 4-km grid
  - `atmosphere` / Synoptic wind variability during simulation: The synoptic wind strength varied over the three-day simulation period. It was weakest on the first day (30 June), allowing mesoscale circulations to develop significantly and trigger convection. The wind was strongest on the second day (1 July), which inhibited the development of mesoscale circulations.
  - `atmosphere` / Convective available potential energy distribution: In the upper-right corner of the domain, covered by uniform forest, a relatively large CAPE of approximately 600 J was present. In contrast, the lower-left corner, also forest-covered, exhibited a much smaller CAPE value of approximately 200 J.
  - `atmosphere` / Cloud enhancement patterns: Enhancement of shallow cumulus clouds near the 800-hPa level was simulated in the afternoon of the first day over deforested areas. Enhanced high cumulus clouds near the 300-hPa level were also simulated over partially cleared areas, contributing to local rainfall.
  - `atmosphere` / Mesoscale heat flux magnitude: The strength of mesoscale circulations at low levels was greatest on the first day in terms of magnitude and depth of corresponding heat flux below 800 hPa. Mesoscale circulations were weakest during the second day, indicated by small values of mesoscale heat flux.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C010` Break period control run with uniform forest
  - `land_surface` / Uniform forest land cover: The simulation utilizes uniform forest land cover conditions, serving as the control run baseline. This contrasts with deforestation runs that use observed patterns of deforestation in Rondônia.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C011` Break period deforestation run with observed patterns
  - `land_surface` / Observed deforestation pattern implementation: The simulation uses observed patterns of deforestation in Rondônia, Amazonia, with scales on the order of 10 km. Land cover data representing deforestation percentage from Skole and Tucker (1993) at 16-km resolution was disaggregated onto a 4 km x 4 km gridded mesh. Forested cells were arranged side by side to mimic fish-bone patterns.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C012` Break period control run with reduced wind
  - `land_surface` / Uniform forest land cover: The control run utilized a uniform forest land cover configuration, serving as the baseline for comparison against deforestation scenarios within the break period simulations under reduced synoptic wind conditions.
  - `atmosphere` / Enhanced mesoscale circulation strength: Under the weakened synoptic wind condition, mesoscale circulations measured by mesoscale heat flux were stronger and reached higher altitudes than the most unstable level of approximately 840 hPa, facilitating the triggering of enhanced cloudiness.
  - `atmosphere` / Reduced atmospheric stability: The atmosphere was less stable compared to the original synoptic wind case, indicated by greater values of Convective Available Potential Energy (CAPE) and smaller values of convective inhibition negative energy (CIN).
  - `atmosphere` / Low-level cumulus cloud characteristics: Enhanced afternoon cloudiness consisted of low-level cumulus clouds observed at levels between 900 and 800 hPa, specifically around the 800-hPa level. These clouds produced little precipitation, with sparse rainfall cells not overlapping with cloud locations.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C013` Break period deforestation run with reduced wind
  - `land_surface` / Deforestation pattern representation: The simulation uses observed deforestation patterns in Rondônia. Land cover data from Skole and Tucker (1993) at 16-km resolution was disaggregated onto the 4 km x 4 km grid. Cells with low deforestation percentages were treated as forested, while higher percentages resulted in mixed or deforested cells arranged to mimic fish-bone patterns.
  - `atmosphere` / Simulated cloudiness enhancement: Under reduced synoptic wind conditions, enhanced cloudiness was simulated over deforested locations. Clouds were observed at levels between 900 and 800 hPa. This enhancement occurred in the afternoon of the second and third simulation days, indicating that mesoscale circulations became strong enough to influence cloud formation when synoptic forcing was weakened.
  - `atmosphere` / Mesoscale circulation strength: Mesoscale circulations, measured by mesoscale heat flux, were stronger under the weakened synoptic wind condition compared to original conditions. The reduced background wind allowed these circulations to develop more significantly, although they still did not trigger deep convection or significant rainfall in this specific break-period simulation.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C014` Break period deforestation run with reduced wind and 2-km grid
  - `spatial_configuration` / Horizontal grid resolution: The simulation was conducted using a horizontal grid resolution of 2 km. This higher resolution was used to check model behavior and resolve processes of clouds and rainfall, which previous studies suggested required resolutions less than 4 km for tropical convection.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C015` Rainy season control run with uniform forest
  - `land_surface` / Uniform forest land cover condition: The simulation utilized uniform forest land cover conditions across the domain. This homogeneous surface configuration serves as the control baseline, distinguishing it from deforestation runs that employ observed heterogeneous patterns.
- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C016` Rainy season deforestation run with observed patterns

- [ ] `R1_P000019:P000019_C020` Dry season deforestation run with observed patterns
  - `land_surface` / Deforestation pattern representation: The simulation uses observed patterns of deforestation in Rondônia, Amazonia, to describe land surface conditions. These patterns include 'fish-bone' structures with scales on the order of 10 km and deforested areas extending up to about 10^3 km^2, consisting of smaller patches.
  - `atmosphere` / Shallow cloud enhancement over deforested areas: Enhancement of shallow cumulus clouds near the 800-hPa level was simulated in the afternoon of the first day and a weaker enhancement on the third day.   - `atmosphere` / Deep convection and rainfall distribution: Localized deep convection occurred over the deforested region on the first afternoon, leading to precipitating high clouds near the 300-hPa level. The resulting rainfall had broader coverage than in the control run, with rain cell locations slightly shifted northwest relative to deforested areas due to prevailing wind.

This effect was most evident on the first day when synoptic wind was weakest, allowing significant circulation development.
