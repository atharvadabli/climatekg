Under strong daytime heating, the presence of cropland or irrigated vegetation is associated with enhanced afternoon cloud cover frequency and a positive diurnal asymmetry, meaning clouds are more frequent in the afternoon than in the morning. [# Impact of mesoscale vegetation heterogeneities on the dynamical and thermodynamic properties of the planetary boundary layer (2009), pp. 1, Claim P000005_CL005; # Impact of mesoscale vegetation heterogeneities on the dynamical and thermodynamic properties of the planetary boundary layer (2009), pp. 9, Claim P000005_CL006]

Contrasting land cover types, such as vegetated versus non-vegetated agricultural areas, can produce a fractional increase in summer daytime cloud frequency of 0.05–0.15 over the wetter or more vegetated patches. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 2, Claim P000014_CL001; # Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 2, Claim P000014_CL005]

Clouds tend to develop earlier in the day and persist longer into the evening over irrigated or vegetated patches compared to surrounding dry agricultural land. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 4, Claim P000014_CL002; # Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 4, Claim P000014_CL006]

Lower albedo and higher surface roughness of vegetated or irrigated surfaces promote efficient exchange of heat and moisture, which enhances thermal convection and moistens the atmospheric boundary layer. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 4,6, Claim P000014_CL004]

Cropland regions exhibit lower evapotranspiration and higher sensible heat fluxes compared to shrubland, leading to warmer planetary boundary layer temperature anomalies that influence local cloud dynamics. [# Impact of mesoscale vegetation heterogeneities on the dynamical and thermodynamic properties of the planetary boundary layer (2009), pp. 7, Claim P000005_CL003]

When synoptic winds are present, the spatial pattern of cloud enhancement can shift to the downwind edges of vegetated patches, particularly at wind speeds greater than 1 m/s. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 4,5, Claim P000014_CL003]

The supplied evidence derives from studies of forests and croplands in different climatic regimes, not directly from semi-arid environments with alternating irrigated and dry patches; transfer to the Rajasthan planning area remains uncertain. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 2, Claim P000014_CL001; # Impact of mesoscale vegetation heterogeneities on the dynamical and thermodynamic properties of the planetary boundary layer (2009), pp. 1, Claim P000005_CL005]

For smaller land cover patches or under variable and weak background winds, edge effects on cloud frequency may be difficult to detect, and some studies found no clear dependence of cloud patterns on wind direction or speed. [# Observational evidence for cloud cover enhancement over western European forests (2016), pp. 5, Claim P000014_CL008]

Satellite climatologies indicate that smaller peaks in cloud cover asymmetry can appear early in the afternoon specifically along cropland boundary edges, suggesting clouds may initiate at patch boundaries before developing into widespread signals later in the day.
