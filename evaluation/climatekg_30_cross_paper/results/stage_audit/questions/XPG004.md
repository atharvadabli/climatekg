## Generated answer

Irrigation increases precipitation in some regions by enhancing evapotranspiration, which raises latent heat fluxes, specific humidity, and relative humidity while cooling the surface and lowering the planetary boundary layer height. This moisture concentration can trigger increased downwind precipitation, such as along the Himalaya ridge in India or over North America and Eurasia. Conversely, irrigation can reduce precipitation in other areas, including parts of India and monsoon Asia, because the resulting surface cooling weakens land-ocean temperature contrasts, thereby suppressing convective rainfall.
