Greater daytime shortwave energy absorption by forests leads to stronger nighttime warming relative to open land, as stored heat is released more slowly due to higher heat capacity. [# Local cooling and warming effects of forests based on satellite observations (2014), pp. 3, Claim P000095_CL008]

In high-latitude northern regions (boreal forests), forests cause a warming effect on land surface temperature relative to open land, primarily due to lower albedo absorbing more shortwave radiation which outweighs negligible evapotranspiration cooling. [# Local cooling and warming effects of forests based on satellite observations (2014), pp. 2,3, Claim P000095_CL002]

In the boreal zone, warmer nighttime temperatures in forested land compared to open land are caused by tall trees enhancing turbulence, which brings heat from aloft to the surface. [Response of surface air temperature to small- scale land clearing across latitudes (2014), pp. 6, Claim P000178_CL006]

Night-time temperatures consistently decrease across mid-latitudes following deforestation, while tropics and polar areas show mixed mild decreases and increases. [# The mark of vegetation change on Earth’s surface energy balance (2000), pp. 2, Claim P000062_CL002]
