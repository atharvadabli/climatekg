## Generated answer

The ~5 km threshold and the 12.8 km optimal scale are not contradictory because they govern distinct physical processes: moisture pooling versus convective triggering timing. The ~5 km scale represents a minimum size required for secondary circulations to isolate dry patches sufficiently to form concentrated moisture pools near the planetary boundary layer top; smaller patches mix the entire layer and prevent this organization. In contrast, the 12.8 km scale produces the fastest transition to deep convection because it optimizes the timing of mesoscale breeze front collisions relative to the diurnal heating cycle (occurring at 11:30 LST), thereby maximizing cloud growth acceleration. Smaller patches (e.g., 6.4 km) cause circulations to decay and collide too early, while larger scales may not align as optimally with the natural transition window.
