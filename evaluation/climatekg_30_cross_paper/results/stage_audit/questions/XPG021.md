## Generated answer

Evidence indicates that the relationship between Amazon deforestation and rainfall reduction is nonlinear; assuming a nonlinear response fitted to observed sensitivities reduces projected precipitation losses by approximately half compared to linear scaling assumptions. While extensive regional deforestation causes significant basin-wide precipitation declines (e.g., 12–21% reductions under business-as-usual scenarios) driven by disrupted moisture recycling, local patch-scale responses can appear negligible or undetectable in coarse-resolution models due to masking by large-scale winds and synoptic variability. Reconciling these scales requires recognizing that while individual patches may not show statistically significant rainfall changes at weather stations (particularly at length scales <20 km), the cumulative effect of extensive deforestation alters regional moisture flux convergence, leading to robust basin-wide precipitation decreases regardless of ENSO phase.
