## Generated answer

The indexed literature does not support a universal recommendation to maximize irrigation for simultaneous cooling, lower heat stress, and more rainfall. While irrigation consistently produces atmospheric and surface cooling and reduces dry heat stress metrics, it can increase moist heat stress, and the supplied evidence lacks any findings regarding rainfall responses. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 4, Claim P000039_CL004; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL009; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL011]

Irrigation application shifts the surface energy budget by decreasing sensible heat flux and increasing latent heat through evaporation. This evaporative cooling reduces land surface temperature and 2-meter air temperature, with cooling effects scaling with irrigation extent. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 4, Claim P000039_CL002; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 4, Claim P000039_CL003; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 4, Claim P000039_CL004; # Warming of hot extremes alleviated by expanding irrigation (2052), pp. 2, Claim P000091_CL002]

The dominant cooling effect lowers extreme dry heat stress and the summer Heat Index, as observed in WRF simulations over regions like the Indo-Gangetic Plain. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL009; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL010]

Conversely, irrigation can induce a shallower atmospheric boundary layer that concentrates moisture near the surface. This enhances low-level moist enthalpy and increases extreme wet-bulb temperatures, which counteracts heat stress reduction under humid conditions. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL011]

The cooling trends are documented in observational data from specific regions (Nebraska, Arkansas, western US) and model simulations (CESM, WRF). Spatial transfer to other locations is not supported due to insufficient local environmental data. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 3, Claim P000007_CL004; # Warming of hot extremes alleviated by expanding irrigation (2052), pp. 2, Claim P000091_CL002; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL010]

The evidence package does not contain any claims addressing the impact of irrigation on rainfall, leaving that component of the query unanswered. Furthermore, the trade-off between reduced dry heat stress and increased wet-bulb temperature limits the universality of irrigation as a heat mitigation strategy.
