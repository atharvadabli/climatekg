## Generated answer

Land-use changes outside a river basin alter internal flow by modifying atmospheric moisture transport; in large basins, precipitation changes driven by external land use can dominate over those from internal recycling. Under steady-state conditions, the resulting change in river flow is determined by the balance between these imported precipitation changes and local exported evaporation changes. [## Remote land use impacts on river flows through atmospheric teleconnections (2017), pp. 6, Claim P000082_CL009; ## Remote land use impacts on river flows through atmospheric teleconnections (2017), pp. 6, Claim P000082_CL010]

Including terrestrial moisture recycling reduces or even reverses estimated river-flow changes because evaporation from land-use alterations returns as precipitation over land, compensating for initial water losses. Accounting for this feedback nearly halves the magnitude of estimated global river flow changes caused by land use. [## Remote land use impacts on river flows through atmospheric teleconnections (2017), pp. 6, Claim P000082_CL006]
