## Generated answer

The spatial extent of land-use-driven evaporation influence ranges from local intensification to regional downwind transport, determined primarily by the type of evaporation and environmental conditions. Interception evaporation, which occurs mainly during wet spells, recycles moisture over shorter distances, thereby intensifying the local hydrological cycle (P000112_CL003, P000112_CL004). In contrast, transpiration remains active during dry spells and seasons, allowing evaporated moisture to be transported over much larger downwind distances where it contributes significantly to precipitation (P000112_CL005). For example, evaporation from upwind irrigated crops contributes a mean of 7% to annual rainfall over rainfed areas in India, with this contribution rising to 15% during the pre-monsoon season and exceeding 20% in the driest years (P000100_CL008, P000100_CL009, P000100_CL011). Vegetation-regulated evaporation is also relatively more important in higher latitudes compared to the tropics (P000072_CL005). However, effects may remain local or negligible under certain conditions; for instance, observed deforestation patterns did not alter rainfall spatial distributions during the rainy season in one study (P000019_CL008), and forest hysteresis contributions to rainfall variability are negligible compared to climate-driven increases in Australasia (P000134_CL015).
