## Generated answer

Irrigation expansion is the most effective land-use intervention for reducing extreme dry heat stress in semi-arid regions, as it consistently lowers high-percentile temperatures even when rainfall is low. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL009; # Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 4,5, Claim P000007_CL008]

Intensifying rainfed cropland does not reliably reduce dry heat under limited groundwater conditions, as drought stress prevents the soil moisture needed for evaporative cooling. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 5, Claim P000007_CL010]

Establishing forest plantations in water-scarce semi-arid watersheds is likely to increase regional temperatures rather than reduce them. [Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL008; Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL010]

Irrigation application enhances evapotranspiration, producing a surface cooling effect that dominates over atmospheric moistening. This mechanism lowers the 95th percentile of daily maximum temperatures and remains effective during drought conditions when rainfed systems fail. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL009; # Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 4,5, Claim P000007_CL008]

Rainfed agricultural intensification relies on adequate soil moisture to realize its increased evapotranspiration potential. When groundwater or precipitation is limited, inadequate soil moisture eliminates this cooling capacity, resulting in no significant temperature reduction. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 5, Claim P000007_CL010]

In water-scarce environments, forest plantations absorb more solar radiation due to darker canopies but cannot substantially increase evapotranspiration due to limited water availability. The net radiative forcing leads to regional warming instead of cooling. [Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL008; Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL010]

The cooling benefits of irrigation depend on a sustained water supply. In watersheds with strictly limited groundwater, maintaining the irrigation volumes required for significant cooling may be impractical. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 4,5, Claim P000007_CL008; Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL010]

Evidence regarding forest plantation warming originates from water-scarce temperate regions. Direct spatial transfer to Indian semi-arid watersheds is constrained by insufficient local geometry and environmental data for relation-family translation. [Protecting climate with forests (2008), pp. 3, Claim P000116_CL008]

Rainfed cropland intensification only demonstrates cooling trends during non-drought periods with adequate soil moisture, making it an unreliable strategy for mitigating strong pre-monsoon heat under water-limited conditions. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 5, Claim P000007_CL009]

Some analyses indicate that rainfed cropland intensification can produce significant cooling trends in summer temperature extremes, but this benefit is strictly conditional on non-drought years and sufficient soil moisture availability. [# Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 5, Claim P000007_CL009]

While irrigation reduces extreme dry heat stress, it simultaneously increases moist heat stress in certain regions, presenting a trade-off between lowering air temperature and increasing humidity. [# Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 2, Claim P000039_CL018]
