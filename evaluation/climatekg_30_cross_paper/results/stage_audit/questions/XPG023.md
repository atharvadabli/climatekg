## Generated answer

A weak background wind (e.g., increasing from 0 to 1 m/s) destroys the symmetry of secondary circulation fronts over heterogeneous wet and dry patches by enhancing downwind motion and deterring upwind motion. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 13, Claim P000018_CL013]

The near-surface convergence zone shifts from the center of the dry patch (under zero wind) to the downwind edge of the dry patch under weak background wind. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 13,18, Claim P000018_CL014]

Clouds initially developing over the dry patch updraft branch are advected downwind, following high-moisture air over the wet patch, rather than remaining stationary as they do under zero-wind conditions. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 13, Claim P000018_CL016]

The ability to reach deep convection is maintained under weak background wind because the potential strength of the secondary circulation exceeds the imposed wind speed, allowing the transition from shallow cumulus to deep precipitating convection to proceed. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 13,18, Claim P000018_CL017]
