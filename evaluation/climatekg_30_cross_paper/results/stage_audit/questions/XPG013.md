## Generated answer

Irrigation in Indian regions increases latent heat flux through enhanced evapotranspiration, which locally reduces sensible heat flux and lowers land surface and 2-meter air temperatures. However, these local cooling effects can trigger remote circulation changes that reduce precipitation in distant regions (such as central-eastern India and the Himalayas) and increase daily maximum temperatures by up to 1°C in those drying areas during September. The distinction between local cooling and remote impacts is evidenced by the spatial decoupling of temperature responses: while irrigated zones experience significant cooling of extreme heat indices, downstream regions suffer warming due to altered atmospheric feedbacks. Furthermore, the consistency of these remote precipitation links varies with climate modes, being less consistent during La Niña years compared to El Niño years.
