## Generated answer

Global and regional models consistently conclude that irrigation cools mean surface air temperatures over irrigated areas (e.g., -0.27 K average impact) by enhancing latent heat fluxes, though this cooling is modest or absent in energy-limited regions like Eastern Asia where background evaporative fractions are already high. Observations support this cooling trend for hot daytime extremes, showing reduced 95th percentile maximum temperatures in areas with rapid irrigation expansion. [# Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres (2017), pp. 12, Claim P000004_CL009; # Irrigation induced surface cooling in the context of modern and increased greenhouse gas forcing (2010), pp. 5, Claim P000037_CL001; # Irrigation induced surface cooling in the context of modern and increased greenhouse gas forcing (2010), pp. 5, Claim P000037_CL006; # Cooling of US Midwest summer temperature extremes from cropland intensification (1910), pp. 2, Claim P000007_CL003]

Studies disagree or add qualifications regarding the net effect on hot extremes when considering all historical forcings; while irrigation alone reduces the likelihood of hot extremes (by a factor of 2–8 in South Asia), its combined effect with other forcings results in virtually no net change in extreme probability globally. Additionally, while irrigation reduces dry heat stress and Heat Index, it increases extreme wet-bulb temperatures due to moisture concentration in shallower boundary layers. [# Warming of hot extremes alleviated by expanding irrigation (2052), pp. 2, Claim P000091_CL001; # Warming of hot extremes alleviated by expanding irrigation (2052), pp. 3, Claim P000091_CL008; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL010; # Moist heat stress extremes in India enhanced by irrigation (1970), pp. 5, Claim P000039_CL011]

A key qualification is that irrigation can cause warming in non-irrigated areas downwind or at a global scale (e.g., +0.079 K annual average over non-irrigated cells) due to atmospheric circulation changes, contrasting with the local cooling observed on irrigated lands. [# Effects of global irrigation on the near-surface climate (2008), pp. 7,11, Claim P000003_CL007]
