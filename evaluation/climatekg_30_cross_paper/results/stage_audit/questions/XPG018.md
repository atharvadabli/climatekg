## Generated answer

A threshold of approximately 5 km is reported for land-surface patch size; exceeding this scale isolates dry patches and triggers a transition from shallow cumulus to deep precipitating convection. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 17,18, Claim P000018_CL008; # The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 3, Claim P000018_CL002]

An intermediate patch size of 12.8 km acts as an optimum, producing the fastest transition from shallow to deep convection, earlier precipitation onset, and higher domain-mean precipitation rates compared to homogeneous surfaces. [# The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 1,8, Claim P000071_CL001; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 1,7, Claim P000071_CL002; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 5, Claim P000071_CL003]

At a large patch size of 51.2 km, domain-mean precipitation increases substantially (by 67% relative to homogeneous conditions) and maximum cloud sizes scale up with the patch size, reaching significantly larger dimensions than over uniform surfaces. [# The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 5,7, Claim P000071_CL006; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 10, Claim P000071_CL007]

Under zero background wind, patches larger than 5 km allow secondary circulations to sufficiently isolate dry patches, preventing whole-planetary boundary layer mixing and enabling concentrated moisture accumulation near the PBL top, which promotes deep convection. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 17,18, Claim P000018_CL008; # The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 3, Claim P000018_CL002]

The 12.8 km intermediate patch size optimizes mesoscale breeze front collision timing (around 11:30 LST), aligning with the natural diurnal transition time to maximize convection acceleration, whereas smaller patches cause circulations to decay and collide too early, hindering optimal cloud growth. [# The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 5,12, Claim P000071_CL012; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 12, Claim P000071_CL009; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 12, Claim P000071_CL011]

Mesoscale circulation strength scales with the size of the surface perturbation, explaining why larger patches generate stronger circulations that enhance both precipitation totals and maximum cloud dimensions. [# The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 5,7, Claim P000071_CL006; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 10, Claim P000071_CL007]

The ~5 km threshold for moisture pooling and convection triggering was observed under zero background wind conditions; applicability to windy environments is unknown. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 17,18, Claim P000018_CL008]

Findings are derived from specific numerical simulations using chessboard-patterned heat flux heterogeneity and discrete patch sizes, limiting generalization to continuous or irregular landscape patterns. [# The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 3, Claim P000018_CL002; # The Influence of Land Surface Heterogeneities on Cloud Size Development (2013), pp. 1,8, Claim P000071_CL001]

While larger patches generally enhance precipitation and cloud size, the relationship is non-monotonic regarding convection timing; intermediate scales maximize acceleration due to optimal breeze front collision timing, whereas both smaller and larger scales yield suboptimal timing relative to the diurnal heating cycle.
