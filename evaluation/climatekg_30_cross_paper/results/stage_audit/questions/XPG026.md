## Generated answer

Patch-edge circulation operates at mesoscales (e.g., 7.5–30 km wavelengths), inducing organized counterrotating cells that transport moisture from wet to dry patches and form concentrated moisture pools near the planetary boundary layer top when patch sizes exceed ~5 km; however, these motions weaken significantly at larger scales (30 km) and are largely independent of initial atmospheric moisture content. [# The Influence of Idealized Heterogeneity on Wet and Dry Planetary Boundary Layers Coupled to the Land Surface (2003), pp. 5,7, Claim P000165_CL005; # The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 10,12, Claim P000018_CL007; # The Effect of Land Surface Heterogeneity and Background Wind on Shallow Cumulus Clouds and the Transition to Deeper Convection (2018), pp. 17,18, Claim P000018_CL008; # The Influence of Idealized Heterogeneity on Wet and Dry Planetary Boundary Layers Coupled to the Land Surface (2003), pp. 8, Claim P000165_CL012; # The Influence of Idealized Heterogeneity on Wet and Dry Planetary Boundary Layers Coupled to the Land Surface (2003), pp. 9, Claim P000165_CL009]

Ordinary downwind moisture advection involves the transport of evaporated moisture over longer distances, particularly via transpiration which remains active during dry spells; this pathway contributes significantly to regional precipitation, with more than 76% of the global land surface receiving at least 10% of its precipitation from upwind vegetation-regulated recycling. [# Contrasting roles of interception and transpiration in the hydrological cycle – Part 2: Moisture recycling (2014), pp. 1,12, Claim P000112_CL005; # Revealing Invisible Water: Moisture Recycling as an Ecosystem Service (2016), pp. 8, Claim P000072_CL010; # Revealing Invisible Water: Moisture Recycling as an Ecosystem Service (2016), pp. 5, Claim P000072_CL002]

Continental moisture recycling is highly significant in the Northern Hemisphere during summer, with approximately 60% of direct evaporation (dominated by interception) returning to land as precipitation; however, estimates of regional recycling rates are sensitive to simulation assumptions regarding convective transport and moisture release altitude, showing contradictory results depending on whether parcels are released low or high. [## Origin and fate of atmospheric moisture over continents (2010), pp. 9,10, Claim P000079_CL002; # Contrasting roles of interception and transpiration in the hydrological cycle – Part 2: Moisture recycling (2014), pp. 12, Claim P000112_CL001; # Forest-rainfall cascades buffer against drought across the Amazon (2003), pp. 7, Claim P000127_CL004; # Forest-rainfall cascades buffer against drought across the Amazon (2003), pp. 7, Claim P000127_CL003; # Forest-rainfall cascades buffer against drought across the Amazon (2003), pp. 7, Claim P000127_CL007]

Translating these pathways into placement recommendations requires evidence linking specific spatial configurations to desired outcomes: for patch-edge circulation, data on how local heterogeneity scales (e.g., >5 km) affect moisture pooling in the user's specific wind regime; for downwind advection, quantification of upwind vegetation contributions to local precipitation deficits; and for continental recycling, resolution of uncertainties regarding convective transport assumptions and regional variability in moisture return rates.
